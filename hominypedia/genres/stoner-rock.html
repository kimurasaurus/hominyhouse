Stoner Rock

Also called stoner metal or stoner doom: doom and psychedelia, groove-led and hazy.

At a glance

Overview

Stoner rock, also known as stoner metal or stoner doom, is a rock fusion genre that combines the weight of doom metal with psychedelic and acid rock. It emerged in the early 1990s and was pioneered foremost by Kyuss and Sleep. Where doom chases dread, stoner rock chases the groove: fat, fuzz-soaked riffs played slow, often with a hazy, sun-baked feel.

Characteristics

Stoner rock is typically slow- to mid-tempo, with heavily distorted, groove-laden, bass-heavy guitars, melodic vocals and deliberately warm, vintage-sounding production. Because it shares so much with sludge metal, the two often cross over, though stoner stays more laid-back and more psychedelic. The tone is built on fuzz, and the tuning is usually low: drop D at the least, often D or C standard.

History

The style's roots run back to Black Sabbath's Master of Reality (1971), along with proto-heavy acts such as Blue Cheer, Leaf Hound, Buffalo and Sir Lord Baltimore. It became a named scene in the early 1990s, when Kyuss emerged from the Palm Desert Scene with Blues for the Red Sun (1992) and San Jose's Sleep released Sleep's Holy Mountain (1992). Monster Magnet's Spine of God (1991) and Fu Manchu followed.

After Kyuss split in 1995, Josh Homme founded The Desert Sessions at Rancho De La Luna and later Queens of the Stone Age, which took the sound to the mainstream with Rated R (2000) and Songs for the Deaf (2002). Labels such as MeteorCity and Small Stone knitted a worldwide scene together: Sweden (Lowrider, Dozer), the UK (Orange Goblin) and beyond.
