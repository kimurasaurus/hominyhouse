Doom Metal

The weight of the riff: slow, thick and heavy with dread.

At a glance

Overview

Doom metal is an extreme subgenre of heavy metal built on slower tempos, low-tuned guitars and a much thicker, heavier sound than other metal. Both the music and the lyrics aim to evoke despair, dread and impending doom. It is strongly influenced by early Black Sabbath, and is the parent of stoner rock and sludge metal alike.

Characteristics

Electric guitar, bass and drums are the core (keyboards sometimes appear), but the structures grow from the blues. Guitarists and bassists down-tune to very low notes and use heavy distortion for a thick tone, often playing the same riff in unison to build a bass-heavy wall of sound. Slow tempos, minor tonality, dissonance (especially the tritone) and repetitive rhythms are hallmarks.

Traditional doom vocalists favour clean, mournful singing (think Ozzy Osbourne's wails or Bobby Liebling's, of Pentagram), while "epic doom" leans operatic and death-doom, black-doom and funeral doom use growls and screams. Lyrically it is gloomy and pessimistic: suffering, depression, fear, grief, death and anger, sometimes with occult or Christian imagery.

History

Doom's earliest seeds include the Beatles' "I Want You (She's So Heavy)" (1969). Black Sabbath are the progenitors: their blues roots, Tony Iommi's doomy guitar and their dark lyrics set the standard. In the early 1970s Black Sabbath and Pentagram were already playing this music; the name "doom metal" arrived in the 1980s, when England's Witchfinder General (Death Penalty, 1982) and the Americans Saint Vitus (1984), Trouble (Psalm 9, 1984), Cirith Ungol (King of the Dead, 1984) and Pentagram (Relentless, 1985) defined it. Sweden's Candlemass (Epicus Doomicus Metallicus, 1986) named epic doom.

The Big Four of Doom

Pentagram, Saint Vitus, Trouble and Candlemass are commonly called the Big Four of doom metal.

Doom grew distinct regional scenes. Finland focuses on the depressive mood of the genre, with very slow tempos and melodic tones; the scene began with Rigor Mortis (later Spiritus Mortis) in 1987, and notable bands include Reverend Bizarre, Thergothon, Skepticism, Shape of Despair and Unholy. New Orleans is the birthplace of sludge metal, where Exhorder first fused doom with punk-influenced metal and Eyehategod, Crowbar, Acid Bath and Down followed. In Washington D.C. and Maryland, kick-started by Pentagram and the Obsessed, the "Maryland doom sound" (also the "Hellhound sound", after Hellhound Records) runs from Saint Vitus, Internal Void, Iron Man and Revelation to Evoken, Spirit Caravan and Earthride. The Pacific Northwest hosts a scene shaped by the Melvins, grunge's rain and forests, crossing into drone and post-metal with YOB, Earth, Sunn O))), Agalloch, Red Fang and Sumac.
