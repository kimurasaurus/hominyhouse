Desert Rock

The Palm Desert Scene: generator parties, free-form jams and the California sun.

At a glance

Cultural originsEarly 1990s, Palm Desert, Southern California, US
Signature tuningC standard · drop C · drop D (Kyuss favoured C standard)

Overview

Desert rock is the hard rock sound of the Palm Desert Scene, a group of related bands and musicians from Palm Desert, California who began playing together in the early 1990s. It mixes heavy metal, psychedelia, blues, punk and grunge, and it is the scene most often identified with stoner rock: the home of Kyuss and, later, Queens of the Stone Age.

Characteristics

Palm Desert music leans on distinctive, repetitive drum beats, a taste for free-form jamming, and "sludgy" or trance-like grooves. Musicians frequently play in several bands at once, so the scene is unusually collaborative. The term "stoner rock" is sometimes used interchangeably with "desert rock", though not all desert bands are stoner rock and vice versa.

History

The scene grew out of desert jam sessions, especially those of Yawning Man, which carried psychedelic and blues influences into long instrumental pieces. Because the isolated desert towns had no clubs, bands built a following at bars and at parties in the open desert. Kyuss in particular played generator parties: small crowds, gasoline generators powering the amps, and no escape if the band was bad.

"There's no clubs here, so you can only play for free. If people don't like you, they'll tell you. You can't suck." Josh Homme, on playing in the desert

In 1992 Kyuss emerged from the scene with Blues for the Red Sun, a founding document of stoner rock. After they split in 1995, Josh Homme founded The Desert Sessions at Rancho De La Luna and then Queens of the Stone Age, while other members spun off into Slo Burn, Unida and more.
