Post-Hard Rock & Metal

Post-metal treats heaviness as texture: dynamics, crescendo and collapse.

At a glance
Also calledPost-metal; art metal, metalgaze; post-hard rock (loosely)
Stylistic originsSludge metal, doom metal, post-rock, post-hardcore, progressive rock, experimental
Cultural origins1990s, United States and England
Signature tuningDrop D · drop C · C standard, down to B standard
Near relativesDoom metal, Sludge metal, progressive rock, post-rock, blackgaze, shoegaze

Overview

Post-metal (and its looser cousin post-hard rock) takes the heaviness and slowness of doom and sludge and rebuilds it around the dynamics and structure of post-rock. Instead of verse and chorus, songs swell, repeat and collapse; heaviness becomes texture rather than a constant.

Characteristics

  • Long tracks built on slow, patient climaxes ("quiet–loud" dynamics).
  • Repetition used for trance and tension rather than hooks.
  • Ambient, drone and electronic textures between the heavy sections.
  • Harsh and clean vocals used as colours, sometimes absent entirely.

History

The term came into use in the 1990s and settled in the 2000s. Its most-cited pioneers are Neurosis, whose shift from hardcore punk toward droning, ambient heaviness on albums such as Through Silver in Blood (1996) laid the groundwork, and Godflesh, whose industrial minimalism pointed another way. Isis, Cult of Luna and Pelican defined the 2000s sound, and the style remains closely entangled with the doom and sludge scenes it grew from.
