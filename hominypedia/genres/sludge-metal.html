Sludge

Doom's filthier sibling, also known as sludge metal or sludge doom.

Overview

Sludge (sludge metal or sludge doom) is an extreme subgenre of heavy metal that combines doom metal with hardcore punk. It generally uses slow tempos, down-tuned guitars and nihilistic lyrics about poverty, drug addiction and pollution. Where doom is mournful, sludge is furious and ugly: it is doom's filthier sibling.

Characteristics

As Metal Hammer's Alex Deller put it, sludge takes "the misery of [doom] and the prodigious narcotic consumption of [stoner metal] while spewing nihilism, self loathing and frenzied punk rock aggression into the vomitous mix." The key traits are slow tempos with heavily distorted guitars, plus hardcore punk's aggression and screamed vocals (though sung vocals appear too). Some bands use industrial, Southern rock and blues elements. The most hardcore-leaning strain is called sludgecore.

History

Sludge began as an act of rebellion against punk's speed. As early as 1982, Flipper's "​(I Saw You) Shine" used dirgey, low tempos that writer Harry Sword called the "genesis of sludge metal". In 1984 Saint Vitus' self-titled debut merged doom with punk in what J. J. Anselmi called "the first sludge metal song on record", and the three-track B-side of Black Flag's My War (1984) is generally credited with beginning the genre. The Melvins then expanded the approach, and were described by Revolver as the band that "invented sludge".

By the 1990s Louisiana had one of the largest and most influential sludge scenes. Eyehategod formed in 1988 when Jimmy Bower and Mike Williams decided to mix equal parts Black Flag and Black Sabbath and "slowed it down as much as possible" to antagonise their peers; their Take as Needed for Pain (1993) inspired a generation. Acid Bath and Crowbar followed, and Eyehategod members went on to be part of other defining New Orleans bands, including Soilent Green and Down. Elsewhere, the Berkeley Gilman Street scene produced Neurosis and Noothgrush, helping pioneer post-metal, while Orange County's Dystopia and Boston's Grief fused sludge with crust and anarcho-punk.
