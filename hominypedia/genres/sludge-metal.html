Overview

Sludge (sludge metal or sludge doom) is an extreme subgenre of heavy metal that combines doom metal with hardcore punk. It generally uses slow tempos, down-tuned guitars and nihilistic lyrics about poverty, drug addiction and pollution. Where doom is mournful, sludge is furious and ugly: it is doom's filthier sibling.

The sound is doom slowed and dirtied by punk: heavily distorted, down-tuned guitars, high aggression and screamed vocals, though sung voices appear too, and many bands add industrial, southern rock or blues elements. The lyrics stay grounded in real-world misfortune addiction, economic disparity, a poisoned earth and often treat it with a sneering, black-humoured irony rare in doom. The most hardcore-leaning strain is called sludgecore, whose birthplace and frontrunner are New Orleans and Eyehategod.

Sludge is one of the wells from which post metal was drawn, and it stands beside doom and stoner rock as a near relative, its heaviest tendencies sometimes bleeding into all three.

Characteristics

As Metal Hammer's Alex Deller put it, sludge takes "the misery of [doom] and the prodigious narcotic consumption of [stoner metal] while spewing nihilism, self loathing and frenzied punk rock aggression into the vomitous mix." The key traits are slow tempos with heavily distorted guitars, plus hardcore punk's aggression and screamed vocals (though sung vocals appear too). Some bands use industrial, Southern rock and blues elements. The most hardcore-leaning strain is called sludgecore.

History

Sludge began as an act of rebellion against punk's speed. Since its inception in the late 1970s, hardcore punk had been defined by high tempos, but by the early-to-mid-1980s a crop of California bands Black Flag, Fang and Flipper above all began slowing down as a way of antagonising the scene. As early as 1982, Flipper's "​(I Saw You) Shine", on Album Generic Flipper, used dirgey, low tempos and expansive length to create what writer Harry Sword called the "genesis of sludge metal". Los Angeles' Saint Vitus, already at the front of the emerging doom scene, recorded their self-titled debut for SST Records; released in 1984, its title track merged doom with punk in what J. J. Anselmi called "the first sludge metal song on record". It was the three-track B-side of Black Flag's My War (1984) and its embrace of Black Sabbath, however, that is generally credited with beginning the genre.

My War's influence took particular hold in Seattle, Washington, where it inspired some of the earliest grunge bands, among them 10 Minute Warning and the U-Men. The Melvins, formed in Montesano, Washington in 1983, were among the most prominent to draw on both My War and Flipper's Album; after seeing Black Flag in Seattle in 1984 they turned from hardcore to slow, heavy riffs, and Revolver later described them as the band that "invented sludge". The grunge scene that grew from the same soil became sludge's most commercially successful moment, as Soundgarden and Nirvana carried a related sound to mainstream audiences in the early 1990s.

By the 1990s Louisiana had one of the largest and most influential sludge scenes. Eyehategod formed in the spring of 1988 when guitarist Jimmy Bower and vocalist Mike Williams decided to mix equal parts Black Flag and Black Sabbath and "slowed it down as much as possible" to antagonise their peers; their Take as Needed for Pain (1993) inspired a generation of bands to form or change their sound. Acid Bath and Crowbar followed, and Eyehategod members went on to be part of other defining New Orleans bands, including Soilent Green and Down. Down's Phil Anselmo later recalled that the Melvins' debut Gluey Porch Treatments "broke the mold, especially in New Orleans", after which "people began to appreciate playing slower".

Elsewhere in California, the 924 Gilman Street punk scene in Berkeley produced a sizeable sludge scene in the late 1980s and into the 1990s, including Neurosis and Noothgrush. Neurosis' move from hardcore to a droning, ambient and progressive style helped pioneer post-metal, a path later joined by Boston's Isis and Umeå's Cult of Luna. Orange County's Dystopia merged sludge with crust punk and grindcore on their debut Human = Garbage, while Boston's Grief did the same with anarcho-punk. In the United Kingdom, Fudge Tunnel and Iron Monkey were the prominent sludge bands of the 1990s, both drawing on the Melvins and the nascent sound.

In 1991 Damad formed in Savannah, Georgia, releasing the albums Rise and Fall (1997) and Burning Cold (2000), which mixed sludge with grindcore-influenced punk. Their influence helped Savannah develop a significant scene in the 2000s around Baroness, Black Tusk and Kylesa, whose blend of punk, metal and rock writers such as J. J. Anselmi called the "Savannah sound". Baroness' progressive take found commercial success in the 2010s, while Atlanta's Mastodon fused sludge with progressive elements on Remission (2002) and Leviathan (2004) before their sound grew steadily more progressive and less sludge.
