Psychedelic Hard Rock & Metal

Including heavy psych: the loud, riffing end of psychedelia.

At a glance
Also calledPsychedelic rock; psych-rock; "heavy psych" for the loud wing
Stylistic originsRock, blues rock, folk rock, garage rock, jazz, Indian classical
Cultural originsMid-1960s, US & UK; heavy psych from the late 1960s
Signature tuningStandard E and drop D; heavy psych often down to C or D standard
Near relativesAcid rock, fuzz rock, space rock, stoner rock, progressive rock, occult rock, heavy psych

Overview

Psychedelic rock is a rock genre that arose in the mid-1960s among musicians aiming to recreate altered states of consciousness in sound: spacey, effects-heavy, improvisational, often surreal. Its heavier, louder wing (sometimes called heavy psych) pushes the same ideas through fuzz and volume, and is one of the two stated origins of stoner rock (the other being doom metal).

Characteristics

Heavy psych (or "heavy psychedelic") is the point where psychedelia gets the distortion, tempo and weight of hard rock and early metal. It blends long-form jamming with fuzz-drenched riffs and often lingers in the same territory as acid rock and stoner rock. It is the direct ancestor of the desert and stoner scenes of the 1990s and of today's retro-psych revival.

History

The genre grew from folk rock and garage rock in the US (the Byrds' "Eight Miles High" is a landmark) and from beat and mod music in the UK. The Beatles' Revolver (1966) and Sgt. Pepper (1967) brought studio experimentation into the mainstream. By 1968–69 the heavier acts (Hendrix, Cream, Iron Butterfly, Blue Cheer, Vanilla Fudge) had turned it into hard, riff-based music, which in turn fed heavy metal, progressive rock and space rock.

A neo-psychedelic revival in the 1980s and a full heavy-psych resurgence in the 2000s, with bands such as Dead Meadow, Comets on Fire, Earthless and King Buffalo, carried the style straight back into the stoner world.
