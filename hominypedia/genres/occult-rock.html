Occult

Ritual and riff: retro hard rock built on occult themes.

At a glance
Also calledOccult rock, doom rock, witch rock
Stylistic originsProto-metal, hard rock, blues, psychedelic rock, progressive rock
Cultural originsLate 1960s to early 1970s, England and the United States; revived from the 2000s
Signature tuningStandard E and drop D; often C or D standard for heavy doom
Near relativesdoom metal, stoner rock, sludge metal, acid rock, psychedelic rock, progressive rock

Overview

Occult rock is rock music whose lyrics, imagery and atmosphere centre on the occult: witchcraft, the esoteric, the ritualistic and the satanic. It first appeared at the end of the 1960s and returned as a distinct retro-rock movement from the late 2000s. It overlaps heavily with doom and stoner rock, sharing their riffs and their sense of dread.

Characteristics

  • Occult, pagan or satanic lyrical themes and stage imagery.
  • Heavy, retro-styled production recalling 1970s hard rock.
  • Organ, effects and doom tempos alongside blues-based riffs.
  • Strong overlap with psychedelia and horror soundtracks.

History

The first wave arrived around the turn of the 1970s: America's Coven released Witchcraft Destroys Minds & Reaps Souls in 1969, often cited as the first occult rock album, and England's Black Widow followed with Sacrifice (1970). Most influentially, Black Sabbath made darkness itself the subject of heavy music.

A retro-occult wave emerged in the late 2000s and 2010s, its figureheads including Ghost, The Devil's Blood, Blood Ceremony, Jex Thoth, Uncle Acid & the Deadbeats, Jess and the Ancient Ones, The Oath and Witchcraft, leaning on vintage gear, analogue recording and theatrical presentation. Coven and Black Widow reformed in 2007 and toured with the bands they had influenced.
