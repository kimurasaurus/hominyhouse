Melodic Death Metal

At a glance
Also calledMelodeath, MDM; Gothenburg metal
Stylistic originsDeath metal, heavy metal, NWOBHM
Cultural originsEarly to mid 1990s, Sweden (Gothenburg) & England
Signature tuningStandard E · drop D · drop C · often drop B on 7-strings
Near relativesDeath-doom, melodic metalcore, melodic black-death; doom metal, post-metal

Overview

Melodic death metal (melodeath, MDM) is a subgenre of death metal that adds the twin-guitar harmonised melodies of the New Wave of British Heavy Metal to death metal's aggression. Over the distorted guitars, fast double-bass drumming and occasional blast beats sit highly melodic riffs and "buzz-saw" guitar tones, and the harsh growls of traditional death metal are often joined by clean singing. Unlike that tradition, melodeath leans more towards verse chorus song structures.

The style is generally more musically diverse than traditional death metal, granting its bands greater freedom in melody and theme. It was pioneered by the English band Carcass with Heartwork (1993) and developed further by the Swedish bands behind the "Gothenburg sound". In the wider extreme-metal family it sits close to death-doom, sharing its melancholy and dark melody.

Characteristics

  • Death growls and harsh vocals over melodic backing.
  • Harmonised, often Iron Maiden-style dual guitar lead lines.
  • Faster, thrashier riffing than doom, but frequent melodic, mid-paced passages.
  • Keyboard and acoustic textures in many later bands.

History

The melodic elements of the style can be traced to traditional Scandinavian musical motifs, and its most direct pioneer was the English band Carcass, which played grindcore on its first two releases before turning to death metal and an increasingly melodic style on Necroticism Descanting the Insalubrious (1991) and Heartwork (1993). The American band Death's Symbolic (1995) is also considered influential, and Sweden's Ceremonial Oath and Eucharist were early melodic death metal bands, though neither gained much attention outside its own scene.

The style's core formed in Gothenburg, Sweden, in the early 1990s. In Flames (Lunar Strain, 1994; The Jester Race, 1996), Dark Tranquillity (Skydancer, 1993; The Gallery, 1995) and At the Gates (Slaughter of the Soul, 1995) defined the "Gothenburg sound"; many of their members shared a single bus route into the city, and the friendships formed around it shaped the scene. In Flames' The Jester Race combined death metal with riffs influenced by Iron Maiden and Judas Priest and made Swedish death metal more accessible, while At the Gates' Slaughter of the Soul brought the style underground popularity, including rotation on MTV's Headbanger's Ball, a nomination at the Swedish Grammys and American tours with Morbid Angel and Napalm Death.

Following their initial success, Gothenburg became the hub of this new direction in death metal; the writer Gary Sharpe-Young called it "the new Tampa", and the genre received a new lease on life. In the late 1990s and early 2000s the style expanded quickly, with Amon Amarth, Arch Enemy, Soilwork, Insomnium, Children of Bodom and the American band The Black Dahlia Murder among the new arrivals. Other styles began drawing on it too, including melodic black-death and, most significantly, melodic metalcore.

In the 2000s melodic death metal reached a wider audience. In Flames' Reroute to Remain (2002) peaked at number 10 on the US Top Heatseekers chart, and its follow-ups Soundtrack to Your Escape (2004) and Come Clarity (2006) reached numbers 145 and 58 on the Billboard 200, the latter winning a Swedish Grammy. The Black Dahlia Murder, Arch Enemy, Children of Bodom and Amon Amarth also entered the Billboard 200 during the decade. At the same time, melodic metalcore became one of the most popular heavy metal genres, with Killswitch Engage often credited with bringing the sound to the mainstream alongside Unearth, Bullet for My Valentine, All That Remains, Shadows Fall and Atreyu. Many of these bands drew heavily on At the Gates' Slaughter of the Soul, to the extent that the phrase "At the Gates worship" was coined for them.

The style continued to spread into the 2000s and beyond. Melodic black-death fused melodeath with black metal and European romanticism, while melodic metalcore combined it with metalcore's breakdowns and clean singing. Some deathcore bands, such as Through the Eyes of the Dead and Bring Me the Horizon, also drew on melodic death metal.
