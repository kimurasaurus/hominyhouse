Melodic Death Metal

Melodeath: death metal sharpened with twin-guitar melody.

At a glance
Also calledMelodeath, MDM; Gothenburg metal
Stylistic originsDeath metal, heavy metal, NWOBHM
Cultural originsEarly to mid 1990s, Sweden (Gothenburg) & England
Signature tuningStandard E · drop D · drop C · often drop B on 7-strings
Near relativesDeath-doom, melodic metalcore, melodic black-death; doom metal, post-metal

Overview

Melodic death metal (melodeath, MDM) is a subgenre of death metal that adds the twin-guitar harmonised melodies of the New Wave of British Heavy Metal to death metal's aggression. In the wider extreme-metal family it sits close to death-doom, sharing its melancholy and dark melody.

Characteristics

  • Death growls and harsh vocals over melodic backing.
  • Harmonised, often Iron Maiden-style dual guitar lead lines.
  • Faster, thrashier riffing than doom, but frequent melodic, mid-paced passages.
  • Keyboard and acoustic textures in many later bands.

History

The style's core formed in Gothenburg, Sweden, in the early 1990s. In Flames (Lunar Strain, 1994; The Jester Race, 1996), Dark Tranquillity (Skydancer, 1993; The Gallery, 1995) and At the Gates (Slaughter of the Soul, 1995) defined the "Gothenburg sound"; in England, Carcass' Heartwork (1993) is often cited as foundational. The style then spread across Scandinavia and fed modern metalcore and melodic extreme metal.
