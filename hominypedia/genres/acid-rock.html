Acid Rock

The loud, heavy wing of 1960s psychedelia, and a direct ancestor of doom and stoner rock.

At a glance
Also calledHeavy psych; often used interchangeably with psychedelic rock
Stylistic originsRock, psychedelia, garage rock, blues, hard rock, psychedelic rock
Cultural originsMid-1960s, United States
Signature tuningStandard E (1960s); drop D common in later revivals
Near relativesPsychedelic rock, hard rock, heavy metal, stoner rock, doom metal, space rock, fuzz rock, progressive rock

Overview

Acid rock is a loosely defined subgenre of psychedelic rock, generally heavier, louder and more improvisational than the rest of the movement. The name is sometimes used interchangeably with psychedelic rock, but it usually signals harder guitar, more distortion and a greater debt to the blues: the ingredients that later hardened into doom and stoner rock.

Characteristics

  • Heavy, distorted, blues-based electric guitar played loud.
  • Long, improvisational tracks, sometimes filling a whole record side.
  • Feedback and volume used as texture rather than accident.
  • Psychedelic imagery and lyrics about altered perception.

History

The label dates from the late 1960s and was applied to bands whose music was bound up with the era's drug culture and whose live shows leaned on volume and free-form jamming. Unlike the more song-oriented psychedelic pop, acid rock put the groove and the riff first. By the early 1970s its heavier wing fed directly into hard rock and heavy metal, and its darkness into doom.

Why it matters here

Acid rock is named as a stylistic origin of stoner rock in its own right. Black Sabbath's early albums grew from exactly this mix of blues, acid-rock heaviness and dread, and Trouble later carried acid rock explicitly into doom.
