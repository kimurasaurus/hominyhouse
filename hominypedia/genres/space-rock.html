Spacerock

Psychedelia turned outward: hypnotic, atmospheric and synthesiser-driven.

At a glance
Also calledSpace rock
Stylistic originsPsychedelia, progressive rock, psychedelic rock, acid rock, space music
Cultural originsLate 1960s and early 1970s, United Kingdom
Signature tuningStandard E or drop D; effects matter more than tuning
Near relativespsychedelic rock, progressive rock, acid rock, stoner rock

Overview

Spacerock (space rock) is a subgenre of rock music, loosely a form of psychedelic rock, built on long, hypnotic, atmospheric pieces with a science-fiction feel. Rather than chase a chorus, it drifts: repetition, echo and synthesisers create a sense of floating through space. It emerged in the late 1960s and has resurfaced repeatedly since.

Characteristics

Space rock favours long instrumental passages over conventional song structure, with heavy use of synthesisers, echo, reverb and delay. Rhythm tends to repeat and evolve rather than resolve, producing a trance-like effect. Lyrics and imagery borrow from astronomy, space travel and science fiction. Because the style lives or dies on texture, bands often keep guitars in standard tuning and let effects, not down-tuning, carry the weight.

History

The genre grew out of the late-1960s psychedelic scene. Early Pink Floyd ("Astronomy Domine", "Interstellar Overdrive") set the template, and London's Hawkwind, who helped popularise the term, fused it with hard rock, volume and sci-fi. Gong and early Amon Düül II pushed it further out; in the 1980s Spacemen 3 rebuilt it around drone and repetition.

A 1990s revival brought the sound back (Monster Magnet, Spiritualized and Flying Saucer Attack among them), and its textures carried into 2000s post-metal and drone. Today it overlaps heavily with stoner rock and the instrumental jam scenes.
